Volunteering in Australia is falling.Where do people still show up?

Millions of people give their time to keep local life moving. The Census says fewer people did so in 2021. These charts look at where volunteering remains strong, who takes part, and what charities report.

Australia runs on unpaid work

From junior sport and surf patrols to community gardens, volunteers help make everyday life work. The Census gives us a five-year snapshot of how many people take part.

2

Where people volunteer

Volunteering happens across the country, but rates vary widely. A place with more volunteers does not always have a higher share of people volunteering.

The headline finding

Every state and territory saw volunteering fall between 2016 and 2021.

Victoria had the largest drop: 5.9 percentage points. The Northern Territory had the smallest: 3.0 points.
3

Fewer people are volunteering

The two Censuses show a clear fall. They count different snapshots of the population, so they cannot tell us whether the same people stopped volunteering.

The 2021 Census was taken during the COVID-19 pandemic. The figures alone cannot separate that effect from a longer-term decline.

4

Who still turns up?

Age and sex shape who reports volunteering. From here on the percentages are worked out against the people who answered the Census question, because the age tables do not come with a population count for each age group.

i

Why do some percentages look a little different?

Maps and regional comparisons use volunteers divided by everyone aged 15 and over. Nationally, that is 14.1% in 2021 and 19.0% in 2016.

The age charts use volunteers divided by people who answered the question, because the age tables do not include population totals. About 6% of people left the question blank. Each chart says which measure it uses; the two are never mixed in one comparison.

5

What volunteers do

Charities report their own volunteer numbers each year. These count roles, not people: help out at two clubs and you appear twice. So the 4.42 million here is not the same thing as the 2.93 million people in the Census.

2.93 million people still volunteered in 2021.

That is 686,103 fewer than in 2016, and every state and territory fell. The people who did turn up were more likely to live outside a capital city — 16.1% of adults there, against 13.2% in the capitals — and more likely to be in their sixties and seventies than in their twenties.
